Honeysuckle Birth Flower: Meaning and June Gift Ideas

Honeysuckle is June's second birth flower, next to the rose. It's a climbing vine with clusters of slender, trumpet-shaped flowers, long curling stamens, and a sweet scent that grows stronger on warm summer evenings.

What honeysuckle means

Honeysuckle stands for devoted affection.

  • Devoted affection and lasting bonds. The vine wraps itself around whatever supports it and holds on, which made it a symbol of love that clings and endures.
  • Happiness and sweetness. The name comes from the drop of sweet nectar at the base of each flower.
  • Generosity. It feeds bees, butterflies and hummingbirds all summer.
Honeysuckle says: "I'm holding on to you."

Folklore

  • Dreams of love. An old belief held that bringing honeysuckle into a girl's bedroom would bring her dreams of love.
  • Good luck at the door. Honeysuckle growing around the front door was said to bring good fortune to the house.
  • A word of care. Some honeysuckle species are invasive in parts of North America, and the berries of many species are mildly toxic. If you plant one, choose a native or non-invasive variety.

Honeysuckle or rose?

  • The rose means love in all its forms and is the most recognized flower in the world. Read the rose guide.
  • Honeysuckle is less expected and more specific: steady, loyal, long-term love. It's a lovely choice for a long marriage, a lifelong friendship, or a parent.
