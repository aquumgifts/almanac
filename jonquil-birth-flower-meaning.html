The jonquil is March's second birth flower, next to the daffodil. It's a smaller, sweeter cousin: several little golden flowers on each stem, narrow rush-like leaves, and a scent strong enough to fill a room.

What the jonquil means

The jonquil stands for affection and desire.

  • Affection. Where the daffodil is cheerful and public, the jonquil feels more personal and tender.
  • Desire and returned love. In Victorian flower language, a jonquil asked for affection in return, a way of saying "I hope you feel the same".
  • Spring and renewal, like every early narcissus.
A jonquil says: "I'm fond of you, and I hope you know it."

Jonquil or daffodil?

Every jonquil is a narcissus, the same family as the daffodil, but not every daffodil is a jonquil. You can tell a jonquil by:

  • Several small flowers per stem, instead of one big trumpet
  • Thin, round, rush-like leaves: the name comes from the Spanish junquillo, "little rush"
  • A much stronger, sweeter fragrance

The daffodil means new beginnings and fits a fresh start or a bright, sunny person. Read the daffodil guide. The jonquil fits someone you love closely: a partner, a sister, a best friend.

March birthday gift ideas with the jonquil

  • Jonquil bulbs to plant in autumn for fragrant flowers every spring. (Like all narcissus, the bulbs are toxic if eaten; keep them from pets.)
  • A jonquil design on something they use daily: a mug, a tote, a shirt.
  • Daffodil and jonquil together, both March flowers, for a gift clearly about their month.
