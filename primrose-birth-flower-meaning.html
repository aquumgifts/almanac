Primrose Birth Flower: Meaning, History and February Gift Ideas

The primrose is February's second birth flower, next to the violet. It's a soft, low flower, usually pale yellow, with five notched petals around a darker eye, and it's one of the very first flowers to open as winter turns to spring.

What the primrose means

The primrose stands for youth and new love.

  • Youth. Its name comes from the Latin prima rosa, "the first rose", because it is among the first flowers of the year. It's a flower of beginnings and of the young.
  • New love. In Victorian flower language, the primrose was a flower of young, tender love, sometimes read as "I can't live without you".
  • Hope after winter. Like other early bloomers, it promises that the cold season is ending.
A primrose says: "Something sweet is just beginning."

A little history

  • Primrose Day. In Britain, April 19 was once marked as Primrose Day in memory of Prime Minister Benjamin Disraeli, whose favorite flower it was said to be.
  • Not the evening primrose. The evening primrose is a different plant with its own meanings. The February birth flower is the common primrose, Primula vulgaris, and its garden cousins.
  • Many colors. Wild primroses are pale yellow, but garden varieties come in pink, purple, red, white and blue.

Primrose or violet?

  • The violet means quiet loyalty: modest, steady and faithful. Read the violet guide.
  • The primrose feels brighter and more youthful. It's a sweet choice for a young February birthday, a new relationship, or a Valentine's gift for someone born in February.

February birthday gift ideas with the primrose

  • A potted primrose. They're sold in late winter and bring instant color indoors.
  • A primrose design on something they use daily: a mug, a tote, a shirt.
  • Violet and primrose together, both February flowers, for a gift that's clearly about their month.
